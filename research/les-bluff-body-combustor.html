Large Eddy Simulations of Spray and Combustion Processes in a Bluff-Body Stabilized High-Speed Combustor

Combustion · Completed
Authors: Kamin Manu, Nhan Truong, Prashant Khare

A ramjet has to atomize, vaporize, mix and ignite liquid fuel within a few milliseconds while the air rushes past at a hundred meters a second. A V-gutter anchors the flame so it does not blow out, but the high temperature, dense spray and shocks make the anchoring region almost impossible to measure directly, which is where LES earns its keep.

The first surprise was the spray behavior near the holder. The droplets do not splash off the gutter as you would expect. They form a thin film that runs along the surface and vaporizes near the hot walls, putting a near-stoichiometric mixture right where the flame sits.

The flame itself wraps both shear layers shed off the gutter and oscillates with the vortex shedding and the longitudinal acoustic modes of the chamber. The grayscale rendering of the temperature gradient makes the flame edge easy to follow.

To quantify the oscillation, I built an OpenCV pipeline that pulls the flame boundary out of the temperature field frame by frame and gives the mean width and RMS fluctuations.

A POD on the temperature field then pulls out the dominant unsteady modes. The leading ones pick up the vortex-shedding signature off the gutter, with the longitudinal acoustic motion of the chamber showing up further down the rank.

How tightly the film-vaporization route couples to the instability modes of the flame is something the post-processing hints at but does not pin down. A thread worth pulling on with a reacting simulation built to ask that question directly.
