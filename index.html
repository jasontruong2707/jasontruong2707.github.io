Research Summary

Welcome to my website, where I share my computational research in aerospace engineering. I am an undergraduate researcher at the University of Cincinnati, working with Dr. Prashant Khare in the Multiphase Flow and Combustion Laboratory, and I am preparing to pursue a PhD in this area.

My work centers on high-speed compressible flows and the physics that governs them: how shocks tear liquid droplets apart, how sprays vaporize and anchor a flame in a ramjet combustor, how swirl sets up the recirculation that keeps that flame alive, and how machine learning can make these simulations run at a fraction of the cost. The common thread is high-fidelity simulation of flows that are difficult or impossible to measure directly.

Below are the four core areas of my research.

Multiphase Flow

Droplet fragmentation, shock-droplet interactions, and gel propellant atomization.
Turbulent Flow

Large eddy simulation of swirling and compressible turbulent flows.
Combustion

Spray combustion, flame stabilization, and combustion instabilities.
Machine Learning

Differentiable solvers and learned numerical schemes that accelerate CFD.
